## The three moments that make the workshop land

1. **Slide 4 — the Icebreaker.** Do not skip it. It surfaces how teachers really feel about
   assessment before you teach anything, and you call back to it on the final slide. The payoff
   line is on slide 51.
2. **Slide 8 — Tasting the Soup.** Ask: *"Does anybody wait until the food is on the table
   before they first taste it?"* Let the room answer. That single question does more than ten
   minutes of explanation.
3. **Slides 39–43 — the Practical Workshop.** The heart of the day. Circulate constantly
   and ask every table two questions: *"What is your learning outcome?"* and *"How will you know a
   good one when you see it?"*

## Timing discipline

- The **break at 00:45** is your anchor. If Sessions 1 and 2 overrun, the whole afternoon
  compresses.
- The **practical workshop needs its full 25 minutes.** If you are behind, cut from Session 4's
  strategy detail, not from the workshop.
- If you are seriously short: skip slides 33–37 (the strategy deep-dives). The quality
  checklist at slide 43 still stands on its own.
- Give the **gallery walk a hard 10 minutes.** It is easy to let it run long because people are
  enjoying it.

## What you need in the room

- A flip chart or A3 sheets — one per group of four to six
- Marker pens and sticky notes for the gallery walk
- Wall space to pin up the group sheets
- The 28-page handbook, printed

## Close with commitment, not summary

Slide 50 asks each teacher to name **one** thing they will do in their next lesson and say it
aloud to the person beside them. Ninety seconds. Spoken commitment to a peer beats a general
exhortation from the front every time.

Then follow up at your next staff meeting. That single question — *how did the commitments
go?* — is what turns a workshop into a change.
