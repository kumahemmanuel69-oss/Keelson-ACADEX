### The three moments that make the workshop land

1. **Slide 7 — Tasting the Soup.** Ask: *"Does anybody wait until the food is on the table before they first taste it?"* Let the room answer. That single question does more than ten minutes of explanation.
2. **Slide 20 — Inclusivity.** Ask: *"When a learner with low vision fails your test, have you discovered they do not know the work — or that they cannot see your paper?"* Let it sit.
3. **Slide 32 — The 70/30 Split.** This is where principles become consequences. Say it plainly: if our 30% cannot be defended, the learner loses, and the school is exposed.

### Three questions to invite from the floor

- Which of the seven pillars does our school break most often?
- Do all of us teaching the same subject use the same criteria? Should we?
- What would it take for us to share rubrics as a department?

### Close with commitment, not summary

Ask each teacher to turn to the person beside them and name **one** thing they will do in their next lesson. Ninety seconds. Spoken commitment to a peer beats a general exhortation from the front every time.
